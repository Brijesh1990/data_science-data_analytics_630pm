# What Is Data? Types and Examples

**Data** is a collection of facts, observations, measurements, or records. It may describe people, objects, events, or processes. Data can be written as numbers or words, or represented as images, sound, video, and other digital forms.

For example, a shop's data might include product names, prices, quantities sold, order dates, customer feedback, and product photos.

## Common Ways to Classify Data

There is no single list that includes every possible type of data. Data is classified in different ways depending on how it is collected, represented, and used. A dataset can fit several categories at once.

### 1. Qualitative and Quantitative Data

- **Qualitative (categorical) data** describes labels, qualities, or groups. Examples: a person's eye color, a product type, or a customer's written feedback.
  - **Nominal:** Categories with no natural order, such as country, color, or blood group.
  - **Ordinal:** Categories with a meaningful order, but not necessarily equal gaps between them, such as small/medium/large or satisfaction levels.
- **Quantitative (numerical) data** represents counts or measurements.
  - **Discrete:** Countable values, usually whole numbers, such as number of orders or students.
  - **Continuous:** Measurements that can take values within a range, including decimals, such as height, temperature, or elapsed time.

### 2. Levels of Measurement

These levels help determine which comparisons and calculations are appropriate:

- **Nominal:** Names or labels only; no ranking (for example, product category).
- **Ordinal:** Ordered values, but the size of the differences is not defined (for example, a star rating).
- **Interval:** Ordered numeric values with equal intervals but no meaningful zero (for example, temperature in Celsius).
- **Ratio:** Ordered numeric values with equal intervals and a meaningful zero (for example, distance, weight, or sales quantity).

### 3. By Structure and Format

- **Structured data:** Follows a predefined format, often rows and columns in a spreadsheet or database. Examples: order IDs, dates, and amounts.
- **Semi-structured data:** Has some organization, such as tags or key-value fields, but not a rigid table structure. Examples: JSON, XML, and many log files.
- **Unstructured data:** Does not follow a predefined tabular structure. Examples: documents, emails, photographs, audio, and video.

### 4. By Source

- **Primary data:** Collected firsthand for a specific purpose, using methods such as surveys, interviews, experiments, or observations.
- **Secondary data:** Already collected by another person or organization, or for a different purpose. Examples: published reports, public census data, or historical records.

### 5. By Time and Observation

- **Cross-sectional data:** Information about multiple subjects at a particular point or period in time. Example: a survey of customers this month.
- **Time-series data:** Observations recorded over time, often at regular intervals. Example: daily temperatures over a year.
- **Panel (longitudinal) data:** Repeated observations of the same subjects over time. Example: yearly income records for the same group of people.

### 6. Common Digital Data Forms

Computers store and process many forms of data, including:

- **Text:** Words, letters, and symbols, such as messages or document contents.
- **Numeric:** Integers and decimal values, such as counts, prices, and measurements.
- **Boolean:** One of two logical values, usually `true` or `false`.
- **Date and time:** Calendar dates, times of day, or timestamps.
- **Binary and multimedia:** Files represented digitally, including images, audio, and video.
- **Geospatial:** Locations and shapes associated with places, such as coordinates or map boundaries.

These are common digital representations; the exact data types available depend on the programming language, database, or file format.

## Summary

Data can be numerical or categorical, structured or unstructured, and collected from different sources or over time. These classifications are not mutually exclusive. For example, daily sales figures are numerical, structured, time-series data collected by a business.
