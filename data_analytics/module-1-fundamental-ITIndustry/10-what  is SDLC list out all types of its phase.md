# What Is SDLC? Phases of the Software Development Life Cycle

**SDLC** stands for **Software Development Life Cycle**. It is a structured process used to plan, build, test, release, and maintain software. Following an SDLC helps teams manage requirements, quality, cost, schedule, and risks.

The names and number of phases can vary between organizations and development methods. The phases below are a common general sequence; in Agile and other iterative methods, teams may repeat several phases in short cycles.

## Common SDLC Phases

1. **Planning and feasibility**
   - Define the problem, goals, scope, budget, schedule, and resources.
   - Assess whether the project is practical and worthwhile, including technical and business feasibility.

2. **Requirements gathering and analysis**
   - Gather and document what users and stakeholders need.
   - Identify functional requirements (what the software should do) and non-functional requirements (such as performance, accessibility, reliability, and security).

3. **System design**
   - Plan how the software will meet the requirements.
   - Define its architecture, components, interfaces, data structures, user experience, and technology choices.

4. **Implementation (development or coding)**
   - Write the code and build the software according to the design.
   - Developers may also perform code reviews and create automated checks.

5. **Testing**
   - Check that the software behaves as expected and meets its requirements.
   - Testing can include unit, integration, system, usability, performance, and security testing.
   - Fix defects and retest affected areas.

6. **Deployment (release)**
   - Make the software available to its users or operating environment.
   - A release may be gradual, such as deploying first to a small group before wider rollout.

7. **Operation and maintenance**
   - Monitor and support the software after release.
   - Correct defects, apply security updates, improve performance, and add or adjust features as needs change.

8. **Retirement (sometimes included)**
   - Plan how to safely discontinue software that is no longer needed.
   - Preserve or migrate required data, inform users, and shut down related services responsibly.

## Common SDLC Models

An SDLC **model** describes how a team organizes and moves through the phases. Common models include:

- **Waterfall:** Phases are usually completed in sequence, with defined deliverables and approvals between stages.
- **V-Model:** Connects each development stage with a corresponding testing stage; testing is planned early.
- **Iterative:** Builds and improves the software through repeated cycles.
- **Incremental:** Delivers the software in smaller functional pieces, or increments.
- **Spiral:** Uses repeated cycles that emphasize identifying and reducing project risks.
- **Agile:** Develops and delivers software in short iterations, incorporating regular feedback and reprioritizing work.
- **DevOps:** Brings development and operations practices together, emphasizing collaboration, automation, continuous delivery, and ongoing monitoring.

These models organize the work differently; they do not necessarily remove the need for requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance.

## Simple Example

For a school attendance application, a team first identifies what teachers need, then designs the application, builds and tests it, releases it to the school, and maintains it based on feedback and technical updates.

**In short:** SDLC is the overall process for building and caring for software. Its common phases are planning, requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance; teams may also include retirement as a final phase.
